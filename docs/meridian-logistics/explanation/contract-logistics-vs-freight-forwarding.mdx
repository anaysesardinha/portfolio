# Contract Logistics vs. Freight Forwarding

:::info Audience
This page is for Account Managers and new hires who need to explain what
Meridian does, and how that differs from a freight forwarder.
:::

People outside the industry often confuse contract logistics with freight
forwarding. The two overlap in vocabulary but solve different problems.

## Contract logistics

Contract logistics is the outsourced, ongoing management of warehousing,
inventory, and distribution under a long-term client contract. A 3PL (a
third-party logistics provider, a company like Meridian) takes on a
client's storage and fulfillment operations: it receives their inventory,
stores it, picks and packs orders, and ships them out, continuously, for
the life of the contract.

Meridian's relationship with a client is structural. We hold their
inventory in our facilities, run their orders through our systems, and
report on their stock levels in real time.

## Freight forwarding

Freight forwarding is transactional, not structural. A freight forwarder
arranges the movement of goods from one point to another, handling
carriers, customs paperwork, and routing for a single shipment or a series
of shipments. A freight forwarder does not typically hold a client's
inventory between shipments, and does not run a client's day-to-day
fulfillment.

## Where the two meet

A single client can use both services at once, and often does. A freight
forwarder might move a client's inventory from an overseas factory to a
Meridian warehouse. From that point on, contract logistics takes over: the
inventory sits in Meridian's storage, under a service level agreement (an
SLA, the performance targets set out in the client contract), until an
order calls for it to move again.
