# Client Account Structure

:::info Audience
This page is for Account Managers and Client Services staff who set up or
manage a client account.
:::

Every Meridian client account rests on three connected documents: a
contract, a service level agreement, and a scope of services. Confusing
the three is a common source of client disputes, so treat them as
separate documents with separate purposes.

## The contract

The contract sets the commercial terms: rates, payment terms, contract
length, and termination conditions. It is a legal document, and Client
Services does not amend it without Legal's review.

## The service level agreement (SLA)

The SLA sets the performance targets Meridian commits to for that
client: how fast an order ships after it is received, how accurate
inventory counts must stay, how quickly a discrepancy gets resolved. SLA
targets are measurable, and ClearLane Visibility (Meridian's client-facing
inventory platform) reports against them in real time, so a client can see
performance without waiting for a monthly report.

## Scope of services

Scope defines what Meridian actually does for the client, beyond core
storage and fulfillment. Two clients can sign nearly identical contracts
and SLAs while having very different scopes, because scope is where
value-added services get added:

- Kitting and assembly
- Labeling and re-packing
- Returns (RMA, return merchandise authorization) processing
- Purchase order (PO) management

A service missing from scope is a service Meridian does not perform for
that client, even if warehouse staff know how to do it for another
account. Checking scope before promising a client something is standard
practice, not excessive caution.

## How the three interact

The contract authorizes the relationship, the SLA measures how well
Meridian performs it, and scope defines what "it" includes. When a client
asks for a new service, Account Managers update scope (and often the
contract) before operations teams start performing it, so the SLA still
means something.
