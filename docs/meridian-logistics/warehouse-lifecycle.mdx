# The Warehouse Lifecycle

:::info[Audience]
This page is for Warehouse Operations staff and new hires learning how
inventory moves through a Meridian site.
:::

Every unit passes through four stages in a Meridian warehouse. Each
stage moves inventory safely to the next one. This flow explains why
any single procedure exists. The four stages flow in one direction:

<TreeDiagram layout="flow" data={lifecycleStages} label="Warehouse lifecycle stages" />

## Inbound receiving

A shipment arrives. You check it against its paperwork and log it into
Wayfinder WMS, Meridian's warehouse management system. You verify
quantity and condition before the shipment moves further.

A mismatch becomes a receiving discrepancy. You resolve these by
following the [process for inbound receipt
discrepancies](/docs/meridian-logistics/process-inbound-receipt-discrepancy),
instead of creating workarounds on the dock.

## Storage

Once received, inventory moves into one of three storage types:

- **Ambient** storage, for goods with no temperature requirement.
- **Climate-monitored** storage, for goods that need a controlled
  temperature range but not full cold-chain handling.
- **Bonded** storage, for goods held under customs control before duty is
  paid.

A _stock keeping unit_ (SKU), the identifier for a distinct product,
uses the storage type required by the client contract and the product
itself. It does not depend on which space happens to be free that day.

## Order picking and pack-out

When an order comes in, Wayfinder WMS generates a pick list using the
picking strategy that SKU requires: FIFO, FEFO, or FMFO, all defined in
the [glossary](/docs/meridian-logistics/reference/glossary). From
there:

1. You pick the items.
2. You pack them.
3. You stage them for outbound.

## Outbound distribution

You verify packed orders against the original order one more time.
Then you hand the order off to the outbound carrier. This final check
catches a pick-and-pack error before it reaches the customer. That is why
it exists as its own step, not an assumed part of packing.
