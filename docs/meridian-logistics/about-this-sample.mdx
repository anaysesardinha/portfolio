# About this sample

:::info Audience
This page is for anyone reviewing this sample, including hiring managers
and other technical writers.
:::

**Meridian Contract Logistics is a fictional company.** Nothing on the
following pages describes a real business, a real client, or real
proprietary material. I built it to demonstrate how I approach internal
operational documentation, using a subject with enough real complexity to
make that demonstration meaningful.

## Why contract logistics

Contract logistics, the outsourced management of warehousing, inventory,
and distribution under a client contract, is genuinely complex. It has
overlapping systems, a specialized vocabulary, and processes where a
missed step has a real operational cost. That combination makes it a good
subject for showing how to turn a messy domain into documentation a team
can run on without me in the room.

## Who this sample is for

The documentation targets internal staff: warehouse operations teams,
account managers, and new hires during onboarding. It assumes a mixed
technical level and is not customer-facing.

## How it is organized

The sample follows the [Diataxis](https://diataxis.fr/) framework, split
into four sections that never mix content types:

- **Explanation** for background a reader needs before acting.
- **How-to guides** for a single task, aimed at someone who already knows
  the basics.
- **Reference** for lookup material: tables and definitions, no narrative.
- **SOPs** for procedures a team must run the same way every time.

The [Contributor Guide](/docs/contributor-guide) states the full set of
standards behind this structure, including how ownership, review cadence,
and cross-linking work.

For the reasoning behind specific choices, such as why this sample uses
one SOP instead of a full library, or how the audience personas were
built, see [My process](/docs/meridian-logistics/my-process).
