# About this sample

:::info[Audience]
This page is for anyone reviewing this sample, including hiring managers
and other technical writers.
:::

Meridian Contract Logistics is a fictional company. Nothing on the
following pages describes a real business, client, or proprietary
material.

I built this sample to show how I approach internal operational
documentation.

## Why contract logistics

Contract logistics is the outsourced management of warehousing,
inventory, and distribution under a client contract. It is complex, in
ways that matter for documentation:

- **Overlapping systems**: a warehouse management system, a client's
  inventory system, and carrier systems all have to stay in sync.
- **Specialized vocabulary**: terms like FIFO, ASN, and bonded storage
  carry precise operational meaning.
- **High stakes**: a missed step can carry a significant operational
  cost.

That combination makes it a good subject. It shows how to turn a messy
domain into clear, repeatable documentation.

## Who this sample is for

The documentation targets internal staff:

- Warehouse operations teams
- Account managers
- New hires during onboarding

It assumes a mixed technical level. It is not customer-facing.

Two habits keep that mix working. Every page states its intended
audience up front, so a reader knows fast whether a page is for them.
Every page defines its domain terms the first time they appear, even
when another page already defined the same term. A new hire never has
to guess a term a warehouse veteran already knows.

I defined these three audiences before writing a single page. [Audience
Personas](/docs/meridian-logistics/reference/audience-personas) lists
their differing needs, which drove the sample's information
architecture.

## How it is organized

The sample follows the [Diataxis](https://diataxis.fr/) framework and
splits into four types that never mix on one page:

- **Explanation** for background a reader needs before acting.
- **How-to guides** for a single task, aimed at someone who already
  knows the basics.
- **Reference** for lookup material: tables and definitions, no
  narrative.
- **SOPs** for procedures a team must run the same way every time.

Diataxis calls its fourth quadrant Tutorials: a lesson that teaches a
beginner by doing. Contract logistics does not teach that way. A new
hire learns receiving and put-away through supervised training on the
floor, not a reading exercise. This sample replaces Tutorials with
SOPs, since the business needs a fixed procedure a team runs the same
way every time, not a lesson.

The [Documentation Standards](/docs/contributor-guide) page states the full set of
standards behind this structure. It covers ownership, review cadence, and
cross-linking.

## Why one of each type, on one connected topic

This sample covers one connected topic, inbound receiving and put-away,
with exactly one page per Diataxis type:

- One explanation page.
- One how-to guide.
- One reference page.
- One SOP.

Two reasons drive that scope:

1. **Volume does not prove a template works.** One fully realized SOP
   proves it as well as four would. A hiring manager needs to see
   consistency, not quantity.
2. **One shared topic lets a reader trace a thread end to end:**

   - The explanation page sets up why receiving and storage work the
     way they do.
   - The how-to guide handles the one failure mode that comes up during
     receiving.
   - The reference page defines where staff store received goods.
   - The SOP ties all of it into one procedure.

Four unrelated topics would show the same template four times, with no
depth on any one process.

The Audience personas page stays in as supporting material. It serves
every topic, not just this one, so it does not count as additional
topic content.
